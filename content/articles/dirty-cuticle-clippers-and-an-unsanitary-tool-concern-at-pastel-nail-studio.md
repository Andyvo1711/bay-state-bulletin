Clients expect nail services to be performed with tools that are visibly clean and prepared appropriately before they touch the skin. This report on Pastel Nail Studio describes a visit that ended before the service could continue after the client saw what she believed were dirty cuticle clippers taken from an opened bag, then watched the technician wipe them with leftover acetone. When the client noticed what appeared to be another person's skin still on the tool, she became uncomfortable enough to leave. The situation highlights how important visible hygiene practices and a professional response are to client trust.

## Clean Tools Should Never Be in Question

The central concern in this experience was the condition of the cuticle clippers. According to the client, they came from an opened bag and appeared dirty, with material she believed was another person's skin still attached. Tools used around cuticles need to be prepared and stored in a way that gives the client confidence they are clean and ready for use. A salon should have a consistent process for handling tools between clients and should never place a questionable tool back into service.

## Wiping a Tool Is Not Reassuring

The client said the technician used some leftover acetone from wiping her nails to clean the clippers before attempting to use them. From the client's perspective, this did not resolve the sanitation concern, particularly because she could still see material on the tool. When a client questions the cleanliness of an implement, the safest customer-service response is not to quickly wipe it and continue. The technician should stop, remove that tool from use, and bring out another properly prepared set. Making the sanitation process visible and consistent can prevent uncertainty before it becomes distrust.

## Take Hygiene Concerns Seriously

When the client pointed out that she could see what she believed was someone else's skin on the clippers, she described the response simply as, "oh ok." She then walked out. A hygiene concern deserves immediate and serious attention. The technician should acknowledge what the client has noticed, apologize for the concern, replace the tool, and explain what is being done before continuing. Even if the issue can be corrected immediately, a dismissive or unclear response can make the client feel that cleanliness is not being treated as a priority.

## Editorial Perspective

This visit shows how quickly trust can disappear when a client questions salon hygiene. The most important improvement is a strict, visible process for preparing and storing tools so there is never uncertainty about whether an implement is ready for a new client. If any tool looks questionable, it should be removed from service rather than wiped down at the station and reused. Finally, when a client raises a sanitation concern, staff should stop, listen, replace the tool, and address the concern professionally before proceeding. Cleanliness should not only be practiced consistently, it should also be clear enough that clients never have to wonder about it.
