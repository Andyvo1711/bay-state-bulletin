Boston University announced an expansion of its financial aid that takes effect for students entering in the 2026-27 academic year.

Admitted first-year students from U.S. families earning under $200,000 a year, with typical assets, will pay no more than $20,000 a year toward the total cost of attendance. Aid covers full tuition and part of housing and dining costs, all need-based packages are loan-free, and families earning $75,000 or less pay nothing.

About 45% of BU undergraduates receive financial aid, and the university awarded $473 million in undergraduate aid last year. "No family should rule out Boston University because they assume it's beyond their reach," said Chris Sedore, interim vice president and associate provost for enrollment and student administration. BU joins other Massachusetts schools that have expanded aid: MIT and Harvard cover costs for families earning under $100,000, and Tufts covers tuition for families earning under $150,000.
