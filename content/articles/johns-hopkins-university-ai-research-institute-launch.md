Johns Hopkins University formally opened its new Institute for Applied Artificial Intelligence this month, a cross-disciplinary research center headquartered on the Homewood campus that will pool faculty from the Whiting School of Engineering, the School of Medicine, and the Bloomberg School of Public Health.

University officials said the institute grew out of years of informal collaboration between computer scientists and clinical researchers at Johns Hopkins, particularly in medical imaging and diagnostic modeling, and was formalized after a multi-year fundraising effort brought in more than $120 million in gifts and research grants.

"We have world-class strength in engineering and world-class strength in medicine sitting a few miles apart, and for a long time those two worlds talked past each other," said the institute's inaugural director, a longtime Hopkins computer science professor. "This gives us a real home for the people who want to work at that intersection instead of doing it on the side."

The institute will fund roughly 25 new faculty and postdoctoral positions over the next five years, with initial research clusters focused on AI applications in early cancer detection, infectious disease modeling, and neurological diagnostics. A new fellowship program will also bring in 40 doctoral students annually, with priority given to projects that pair a computer science advisor with a clinical or public health co-advisor.

Baltimore city officials have welcomed the announcement as a boost to the region's biotech and technology sector, noting that Johns Hopkins remains the state's largest private employer and a major source of spinout companies in the Baltimore–Washington corridor. University leaders said the institute's new building, a renovated former warehouse space near the Homewood campus, is expected to open to researchers by early next year.
