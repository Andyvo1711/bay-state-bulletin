Employment among cybersecurity and intelligence contractors clustered near Fort Meade continues growing steadily, according to a regional economic development report, reinforcing Anne Arundel and Howard counties' status as one of the country's densest concentrations of national security technology work.

The area surrounding Fort Meade, home to the National Security Agency and U.S. Cyber Command, has developed an extensive ecosystem of contractors and subcontractors performing cybersecurity, signals intelligence, and software development work, with employment in the sector growing even as broader federal spending debates have created uncertainty elsewhere in the government contracting industry.

"This region has a talent pipeline and a security clearance infrastructure that's very difficult for other parts of the country to replicate quickly, and that's kept demand for office and lab space here strong even when federal contracting overall has faced headwinds," said the author of the regional economic development report. "Companies that need cleared cybersecurity talent don't have many other places to go."

The report found continued commercial real estate development in the corridor connecting Fort Meade to Baltimore and Washington, with several new office campuses under construction specifically designed to accommodate secure facility requirements that cybersecurity and intelligence contractors need for classified work. Local officials said the sector has proven more resilient to broader economic downturns than other parts of the regional economy, given the relatively stable nature of national security spending.

County economic development officials said they continue marketing the region specifically to companies seeking proximity to Fort Meade, citing the area's existing cleared workforce as a key selling point that new entrants to the cybersecurity contracting industry cannot quickly replicate elsewhere.
