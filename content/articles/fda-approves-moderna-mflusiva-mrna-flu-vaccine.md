The U.S. Food and Drug Administration approved Moderna's mRNA seasonal influenza vaccine, mFlusiva, in early August, making it the first mRNA-based flu vaccine approved in the United States. The company is based in Cambridge, Massachusetts.

The vaccine is indicated for the prevention of influenza A and B in adults 50 and older. Approval for ages 50-64 is traditional, based on a randomized Phase 3 trial of 40,805 adults across 11 countries that found 27% fewer flu cases than with a comparison vaccine. Approval for ages 65 and up is accelerated, and Moderna will run a postmarketing trial to confirm benefit. No major safety issues were reported, though temporary reactions such as injection-site pain and fever occurred somewhat more often than with current flu shots.

The FDA had initially declined to review the application, then reversed course after Moderna publicly challenged the decision, and an advisory panel unanimously recommended approval in June. Moderna expects the vaccine to be available for the 2026-27 respiratory virus season, pending CDC recommendations.
