Massachusetts voters narrowly rejected Question 7, which would have required every city and town to allow single-family homes on lots as small as 5,000 square feet. The Associated Press called the race November 6, days after the November 3 election.

One count put the tally at 1,290,724 no votes (50.6%) to 1,258,029 yes votes (49.4%), a margin of roughly 32,700. The lots also needed public sewer and water and at least 50 feet of frontage. Support was strongest in Boston and the cities and suburbs around it, while many smaller and more distant towns voted no.

Voters on other November 3 questions approved extending the public records law to the Legislature and governor's office, allowing public-defender employees to unionize and Election Day voter registration, and rejected eliminating party primaries, repealing recreational marijuana and repealing the state's 2024 gun law.
