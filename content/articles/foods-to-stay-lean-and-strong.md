Staying lean is less about eliminating foods than about choosing ones that keep you full, support muscle and fit your calorie needs.

**Protein at every meal.** Greek yogurt, eggs, fish, poultry, tofu, beans and lentils help preserve muscle, especially when combined with strength training, and tend to be more filling than carbohydrate-only snacks.

**Fiber-rich foods.** Vegetables, fruit, oats, beans and whole grains add volume and slow digestion, which can help with fullness.

**Berries and whole fruit.** Fruit satisfies a sweet craving with fiber, water and vitamins, and blueberries and strawberries are lower in sugar than many desserts. Eating whole fruit is generally better than drinking juice.

**Healthy fats in moderation.** Nuts, olive oil and avocado are nutritious but calorie-dense, so portions matter.

**Water over sugary drinks.** Swapping sweetened drinks for water or unsweetened tea is one of the simplest ways to reduce calories.

**Habits that matter.** Regular meals, enough sleep, limited ultra-processed snacks and consistent physical activity matter as much as any single food. Crash diets can lead to muscle loss and are difficult to sustain.

Individual needs vary with age, health and activity level. A registered dietitian can set realistic goals, and anyone with a medical condition should check with their doctor first.

*This article is general wellness information, not medical advice. Talk with your doctor or a registered dietitian before changing your diet, especially if you have a medical condition or take medication.*
