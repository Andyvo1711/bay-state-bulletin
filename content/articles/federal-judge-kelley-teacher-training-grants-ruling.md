A federal judge in Boston ruled Thursday that a U.S. Department of Education directive used to cancel about $600 million in teacher-training grants violated federal law.

U.S. District Judge Angel Kelley found the department's February 2025 directive was "arbitrary and capricious" and not in accordance with the Administrative Procedure Act. The department had said the grants supported "divisive ideologies," including diversity, equity and inclusion efforts. The cuts reached the Teacher Quality Partnership and Supporting Effective Educator Development programs, affecting more than 100 programs.

Massachusetts was one of eight Democratic-led states that sued over the cuts. Judge Kelley wrote that "the reliance interests are many, substantial, and deep-rooted," referring to the states, students, teachers and principals who depend on the grants.

The ruling does not restore the money. The judge said arguments about recovering past funding belong in the Court of Federal Claims. In April 2025, the U.S. Supreme Court allowed the government to proceed with the cuts while the case continued, after a federal judge in Boston had temporarily halted them in March 2025 and the federal appeals court in Boston had rejected the administration's bid to resume them. The Education Department did not immediately respond to a request for comment.
