New Bedford's fishing industry, which has ranked as the nation's top-grossing fishing port for over two decades on the strength of its scallop fleet, is adjusting its overseas sales strategy as shifting international trade rules affect access to several key export markets that have historically purchased significant volumes of New Bedford seafood.

Industry representatives said several seafood processors and exporters have diversified their export customer base over the past few years in response to periodic trade disputes and changing tariff arrangements, reducing reliance on any single overseas market that could be disrupted by a future trade policy shift.

"Ten years ago, a handful of countries bought the overwhelming majority of our scallop export volume, and when trade relations with any one of them got complicated, the whole fleet felt it immediately," said an economist with a regional seafood industry trade group. "Processors and exporters have gotten a lot more deliberate about spreading that risk across more markets since then."

The fishing industry remains one of the largest economic drivers in New Bedford, supporting processing plants, a substantial trucking and cold-chain logistics network, and thousands of jobs directly tied to the scallop and groundfish fleets. Industry representatives said export markets remain an important outlet for high-value catch like sea scallops, making export access a persistent priority for the industry's profitability.

State fisheries officials said they continue working with federal trade representatives to maintain and expand market access for New Bedford's fishing fleet, describing the industry as economically vital to the South Coast region despite representing a relatively small share of the state's overall economy.
