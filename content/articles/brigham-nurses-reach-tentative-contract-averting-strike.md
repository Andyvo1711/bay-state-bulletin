Brigham and Women's Hospital nurses and Mass General Brigham reached a tentative contract agreement Wednesday, November 11, averting an open-ended strike set to begin the next day.

The union said its bargaining committee will recommend that the more than 4,000 nurses ratify the agreement, which runs from ratification through September 30, 2028. The hospital committed in contract language that it does not intend to replace permanent Brigham nurses with temporary nurses, and it will not use the hundreds of temporary workers it had hired and begun training. Reporting said the deal addresses the union's priorities on traveling and temporary nurses, affordable health insurance and the wage scale, and the proposed contract calls for 20 new 36-hour positions in the hospital's float pool.

Brigham said the agreement fell within the limits of its last, best and final offer presented October 10. The dispute included a July 8 one-day strike and four-day lockout. As of publication, no ratification vote had been reported.
