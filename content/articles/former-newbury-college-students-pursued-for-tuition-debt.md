Former students of Newbury College, the Brookline school that closed in 2019, are still being pursued for unpaid tuition, WGBH reported November 2.

Cesarina Barros left in 2009 owing about $10,000, and in summer 2025 the college sued her for about $28,000 in back tuition and interest. Court documents filed in Boston Municipal Court show that Barros and 20 other former students have been threatened with wage garnishment.

In 2022, the Massachusetts Supreme Judicial Court and the attorney general's office named Wheaton College in Norton as the legal successor to Newbury's funds and assets. Wheaton says it did not acquire any student accounts or outstanding balances, and says the inherited fund has grown to $4 million and has benefited seven students. Court filings by the debt collector, however, list both Newbury's former Brookline address and Wheaton's address in Norton.

A consumer lawyer quoted in the report said whether a closed college's direct tuition claims must be repaid depends on how the school's affairs were wound down and where its debts went. Some states, including Maryland and Washington, have moved to prohibit collecting debt from students at colleges that close abruptly.
