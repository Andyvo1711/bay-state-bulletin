# The "Price Gouging" Crisis and Severe Medical Liability: When a Pedicure Ends in Surgery

**Customer Service Bulletin – Legal Liability & Service Consultation Ethics Feature**

According to a customer review involving **Hermes T.Salon At James Run**, what began as a routine manicure and pedicure reportedly turned into a costly and medically serious experience. The reviewer described concerns about unexpected charges, injuries sustained during the service, and dissatisfaction with the salon's response afterward. These are allegations made by the reviewer and should not be interpreted as established fact.

## The Upsell Trap

According to the review, the customer was initially quoted approximately **$120** for a manicure and pedicure. During the appointment, the technician allegedly recommended switching to a different product, describing it as a better option without clearly explaining the additional cost.

At checkout, the customer said the final bill totaled **$206**, leaving her feeling surprised and believing she had not been adequately informed before agreeing to the upgrade.

## Reported Medical Complications

The reviewer further alleged that an electric nail drill was used aggressively around the cuticle area, resulting in cuts. According to the customer, those injuries later became infected, requiring medical treatment.

The review states that the customer sought professional medical care, received antibiotics, and ultimately underwent surgical removal of four toenails. While this account reflects the reviewer's experience, it has not been independently verified.

## Concerns About the Salon's Response

According to the customer, she contacted the salon to explain the medical situation and request assistance.

The reviewer claimed management referred to a no-refund policy and requested she return to the salon for evaluation. She further alleged that after explaining she had already been treated by a physician and was unable to return immediately, she felt her concerns were dismissed.

The review also alleged that one employee criticized another employee's qualifications during the appointment, creating an impression of internal conflict and inconsistent professional standards.

## Editorial Perspective

Whether or not every allegation can be substantiated, the issues raised illustrate several customer service principles that apply across the beauty industry.

Transparent pricing should always precede any service upgrade, particularly when costs increase substantially. Likewise, sanitation, technician training, and safe use of professional tools remain fundamental responsibilities for salons.

When a customer reports a possible injury associated with a service, empathetic communication, careful documentation, and prompt investigation can help protect both clients and businesses while demonstrating professionalism.

## Key Takeaways

- Clearly disclose upgrade pricing before performing additional services.
- Prioritize sanitation and proper technician training.
- Respond professionally to reports of injuries or adverse outcomes.
- Avoid internal staff disputes in front of customers.
- Transparent communication helps preserve customer trust.

## Final Thoughts

The allegations described in this customer review highlight how pricing transparency, technical execution, and post-service communication can all influence public perception of a business. Regardless of the final facts surrounding any individual incident, beauty professionals who emphasize safety, honesty, and accountability are generally better positioned to maintain long-term customer confidence.
