Massachusetts students' scores on the Massachusetts Comprehensive Assessment System (MCAS) slipped again this year, state education officials said Tuesday, continuing a decline that began with the pandemic.

In grades 3 through 8, 40% of students met or exceeded expectations in English language arts, down 2 percentage points from 2025 and well below the 52% recorded in 2019. In math, 41% met standards, unchanged from last year but still short of the 49% seen before the pandemic. Fourteen districts returned to or exceeded their pre-pandemic performance in both subjects.

Tenth-grade results fell further. In English, 45% of students met standards, down from 51% in 2025 and 61% before the pandemic. In math, 44% did, compared with 45% last year and 59% pre-pandemic. Science scores were mixed: 43% in elementary grades, 39% in middle school (up 2 points) and 41% in high school (down 5 points).

"We're still not where we want to be in terms of our results," Education Commissioner Pedro Martinez said. Jack Schneider, director of the UMass Amherst Center for Education Policy, said the underlying theme remains that "we experienced a global pandemic in 2020."

The results will not affect diplomas. Voters in 2024 removed MCAS as a graduation requirement, and a state council's late-2025 proposal for new graduation requirements, including end-of-course exams, has drawn opposition from the Massachusetts Teachers Association, which calls them duplicative of MCAS. Business and education advocates have urged the Healey administration to press for such tests.
