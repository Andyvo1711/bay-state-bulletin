Few moments in a salon visit matter as much as the one at the front desk. A client of Top Nails recently shared that they left the salon "really upset" after seeing the final bill. According to their account, an extra charge for French tips appeared on top of the fill they had come in for, and the total was higher than they had planned. The review is a useful reminder that even a good service can end on a sour note when the price is a surprise.

## When an Add-On Appears Only at Checkout

The client explained that they had never paid a separate fee for French tips in the past, so a charge for it came as a surprise. They described it as an extra $25 on top of the fill, and said they felt the total of $71 was "too much for no design." In their perception, the service did not include any nail art, so the final amount felt out of step with what they received.

Add-ons such as French tips, chrome, or extra shaping are common in the industry, and many salons price them separately. Problems tend to begin when the client learns about the fee for the first time at the register. Clients who have visited other salons, or even the same salon on a different day, may have different expectations about what a base price includes. The most reliable fix is to say it before the work begins. A technician can simply confirm the plan out loud: "A fill with French tips is a fill plus a French add-on, and the total will be about this much. Does that work for you?" That single sentence protects the client from surprises and protects the salon from misunderstandings.

## Making the Price List Easy to See

The client's advice to others was to ask what the salon will charge before starting. That is thoughtful advice, but it places the burden on the client. A better system makes the answer visible without anyone needing to ask.

A clear, printed menu at each station that lists common add-ons, a note that French tips, nail art, and shaping are priced separately, and a short digital or laminated price sheet at the front desk all help. When the price list matches what the client sees at checkout, there is little room for confusion. It also helps to update the menu whenever prices change, so that regular clients are never comparing today's bill to an older memory. Front desk staff can support the process by reading the itemized total aloud before payment, which gives clients a chance to ask questions while the conversation is still friendly.

## Handling the Moment When a Client Is Surprised

Once a client is upset at the register, the response matters as much as the pricing itself. A calm, respectful explanation of each line item, offered without defensiveness, often settles the situation. If the misunderstanding started with unclear communication, a manager may decide to adjust the charge as a goodwill gesture, and that decision can be worth far more than the amount involved. Thanking the client for raising the concern and inviting them to share how the salon could make prices clearer shows that feedback is taken seriously.

## Editorial Perspective

This case shows that the value of a service is judged twice: once in the chair and again at the register. Even a well-done fill can leave a client feeling shortchanged if the final number did not match their expectation.

First, confirm add-ons and the estimated total before the service begins, so that every charge is a shared decision. Second, keep the menu visible and current, so clients do not have to ask what things cost. Third, when a client is surprised, listen first, explain each line calmly, and consider a goodwill adjustment when the communication gap started on the salon's side. Salons that treat price clarity as part of hospitality give clients a reason to return without hesitation.
