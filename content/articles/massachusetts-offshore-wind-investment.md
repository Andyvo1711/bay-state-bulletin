Massachusetts's major electric utilities are significantly increasing investment in grid infrastructure, filing new rate cases with state regulators to fund transmission upgrades ahead of several large offshore wind projects scheduled to begin delivering power off the South Coast over the coming years.

The investment plans would add substantial new transmission capacity connecting planned offshore wind interconnection points to the broader New England grid, with utility officials saying the projects' combined generating capacity requires grid upgrades well beyond what the region has historically needed to support.

"These offshore wind projects can deliver an enormous amount of power, but only if the onshore grid infrastructure exists to actually move that electricity where it's needed," said a utility executive overseeing the transmission investment. "If we don't get ahead of this, we risk becoming the reason these projects get delayed or curtailed."

The proposed investments would be recovered gradually through utility rates, a funding mechanism that has drawn some pushback from consumer advocates who argue that ratepayers should not bear a disproportionate share of infrastructure costs tied to projects still working through permitting and construction delays. Utility officials countered that the offshore wind buildout benefits the grid over the long term by diversifying the state's generation mix away from natural gas.

State energy regulators are reviewing the rate case proposals and are expected to issue a decision within the next several months. Clean energy advocacy groups have generally supported the investment plans, arguing that transmission readiness remains one of the most significant factors in whether Massachusetts meets its offshore wind and climate targets on schedule.
