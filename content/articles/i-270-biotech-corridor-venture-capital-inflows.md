Venture capital investment in Maryland's I-270 biotech corridor, stretching from Bethesda through Gaithersburg to Frederick, reached its highest level in five years last quarter, according to data compiled by a regional life sciences trade group, driven largely by continued investor interest in cell and gene therapy startups.

The corridor, long anchored by the National Institutes of Health and the FDA's headquarters in White Oak, has developed one of the country's densest concentrations of biotech companies spun out of federal research, and investment data shows funding increasingly concentrated in later-stage companies moving therapies toward clinical trials rather than earliest-stage seed deals.

"What's different about this corridor compared with somewhere like Boston or the Bay Area is how much of the early science traces directly back to NIH labs," said the trade group's chief economist, who compiled the funding report. "Investors have started to recognize that pipeline as a genuine competitive advantage rather than treating it as a secondary biotech hub."

The report found that Maryland-based biotech companies raised significantly more in later-stage funding rounds compared with the prior year, even as overall national venture funding for early-stage biotech deals remained relatively flat. Several corridor companies completed funding rounds exceeding $100 million during the period, a threshold report authors said Maryland companies rarely reached as recently as five years ago.

State economic development officials have pointed to the funding data as validation of continued public investment in biotech incentives, including tax credits for life sciences companies that locate or expand within the state. Industry representatives said the corridor's continued growth will depend partly on whether Maryland can keep pace with laboratory space construction to accommodate expanding companies.
