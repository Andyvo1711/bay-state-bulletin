A customer who visited VIP Nail Spa says she left unhappy with the shape of her nails, and that the problems only got worse in the days that followed. Her account describes a set that looked uneven from the start and then failed outright soon after, ending in a painful experience she says she does not want to repeat. This case is worth examining for what it suggests about shaping accuracy and how well a set holds up once a client leaves the chair.

## Crooked Shaping That Was Visible Right Away

The client says her nails looked crooked and that the overall result did not meet her expectations for the appointment. When shaping comes out uneven, it usually traces back to inconsistent filing technique across the set, not checking each nail against its neighbors as the shape is finalized, or moving through the service too quickly to catch small asymmetries before they become noticeable. A simple habit of holding the client's hand at arm's length periodically during shaping, the way she would see it herself, helps catch unevenness while it is still easy to correct, rather than after the polish or product has already been applied.

## Peeling and Painful Breakage in the Days After

Beyond the shape, the client says the finish began peeling after only a couple of days, and that three of her nails ultimately snapped off, which she describes as one of the most painful things she has experienced. This is presented here as the client's own account of what happened, since the precise cause of the breakage cannot be confirmed without examining the nails directly. Even so, product peeling shortly after application often points to issues with nail prep, such as oils or moisture left on the nail plate, insufficient buffing to help the product adhere, or curing time that was cut short. When a set fails this early and this severely, it is worth reviewing each step of the prep and application process rather than assuming the client's daily activities alone are to blame. Taking a client's report of pain seriously, and offering to have the set safely removed and assessed rather than leaving her to deal with breakage on her own, also matters for how the experience is remembered.

## Editorial Perspective

This case offers a few takeaways for salon teams. First, check shaping symmetry across the whole set as the service progresses, not only at the very end, so unevenness can be corrected while it is still simple to fix. Second, when nails peel or break shortly after a service, treat it as a signal to review the prep and application steps rather than a one-off. Third, when a client describes pain from a service outcome, respond with care and a clear offer to help, since how a salon handles the aftermath often shapes whether a client is willing to give it another chance.
