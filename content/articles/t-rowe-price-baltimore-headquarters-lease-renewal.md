T. Rowe Price, the Baltimore-based investment management firm, signed a long-term lease renewal for its downtown headquarters this week, easing years of speculation among city officials about whether the company might eventually relocate its headquarters outside Baltimore.

The renewal comes after the firm had reduced its office footprint slightly in recent years amid broader hybrid work trends across the financial services industry, a shift that had fueled concern among city economic development officials given T. Rowe Price's role as one of downtown Baltimore's largest private employers and a significant source of the city's commercial tax base.

"This company has been headquartered in Baltimore for nearly ninety years, and this lease is a strong signal that this remains where the company sees its long-term future," said a city economic development official involved in discussions with the firm. "Losing a headquarters of this size would have been a significant blow to downtown's office market."

The firm's continued presence is expected to help stabilize downtown Baltimore's office market, which like many urban cores has faced elevated vacancy rates as hybrid work has reduced overall office space demand. T. Rowe Price said it employs several thousand people at its Baltimore headquarters, with additional employees working across satellite offices nationally and internationally.

City officials said the lease renewal was supported in part by state and local economic incentive discussions, though details of any specific incentive package were not disclosed. Downtown business groups welcomed the announcement as a positive signal for a commercial office market that has struggled with vacancy since the pandemic reshaped office attendance patterns.
