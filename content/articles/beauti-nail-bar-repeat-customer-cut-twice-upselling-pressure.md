A client who had visited Beauti Nail Bar three times described her most recent appointment as a noticeable step down from her earlier visits. What started as a routine manicure turned into an experience she found upsetting, one that offers a useful lesson for salons on staying consistent with returning customers.

## Pressure to Upsell During the Appointment

According to the client, the manager who handled her nails that day came across as pushy, repeatedly steering her toward pricier services. She explained that she had clearly stated what she wanted for her nails before the service began. Despite this, she said the technician's approach felt less like following her instructions and more like an attempt to upsell additional treatments. She also noted a shift in demeanor during the appointment, describing what she felt was an attitude from the manager as the session progressed. When staff focus more on maximizing the sale than on listening to the client's request, it can leave customers feeling like a transaction rather than a valued guest. Clear communication training, especially around respecting a client's stated preferences the first time they are given, can help avoid this impression, even when upselling itself is a legitimate part of the business model.

## Reported Skin Injuries and a Drop in Quality

The client also said that during the service, her skin was cut twice. She noted that this was unusual for her experience at Beauti Nail Bar, since her three prior visits had not included anything similar. She added that the finished manicure did not meet the standard she was accustomed to, describing it as noticeably different from her earlier appointments. For a salon, an accidental nick can happen occasionally, but when it happens more than once in a single visit, it signals a need to slow down and refocus on technique, tools, and client comfort. Consistency matters just as much for returning customers as it does for first time visitors. Building in a habit of checking in with the client mid-service, especially when something feels rushed or off, can catch these issues before the appointment ends.

## Editorial Perspective

First, the case underscores that consistency, not just skill on a single visit, is what keeps repeat customers coming back; a longtime client's shifting perception of a salon usually reflects a real change in how the service was delivered. Second, upselling needs to be handled with care. A client who has already stated her preference should feel heard, not pressured to reconsider mid-appointment. Third, physical safety during any nail service should take priority over speed, particularly when a client raises concerns about being nicked or cut, since repeated small injuries can quickly erode the trust that took years to build.
