Nurses at Brigham and Women's Hospital voted Thursday to authorize an open-ended strike amid stalled contract talks with Mass General Brigham, the Massachusetts Nurses Association said.

The union reported 2,986 votes in favor and 215 against, or 93% support, calling it the largest strike authorization by nurses in state history. No strike date has been set, and nurses must give 10 days' notice. "Brigham nurses have made clear that we are prepared to stay on strike for as long as it takes to win a contract," said Kelly Morgan, a labor and delivery nurse and chair of the bargaining committee.

In July, a planned one-day strike grew into a five-day work stoppage after Mass General Brigham brought in temporary replacement nurses. Nurses' demands include cost-of-living increases, affordable health insurance, limits on temporary travel nurses and patient care protections. Mass General Brigham says nurses receive 5% annual raises on a 20-step wage scale, that it proposed a 2.5% increase for top-step nurses, and that 11 nurses in the bargaining unit earned more than $300,000 last year.

Boston Medical Center nurses also voted earlier this month to authorize a three-day strike.
