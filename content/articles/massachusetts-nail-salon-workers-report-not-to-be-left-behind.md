A report by the Massachusetts Nail Salon Project, a coalition of advocacy groups, finds nail salon workers face health risks, financial strain and discrimination. The survey covered 95 workers and 47 owners in an industry of more than 18,000 licensed technicians in about 1,700 salons, mostly Asian immigrant women.

About 80% of workers earn less than $40,000 a year, and almost a third said they feel unsafe at work because of their identity or race. Workers report headaches, nausea and vomiting from chemicals such as acetone and formaldehyde. Lan Nguyen, 55, a Dorchester nail technician for 20 years, said her roughly $30,000 income is "not too much money to survive for me and the three kids."

"We cannot expect salon owners and workers to fix systemic problems in the industry on their own," said Thao Ho of Greater Boston Legal Services' Asian Outreach Center, a coauthor. The report recommends better ventilation, state-sponsored English classes, including nail salons in future regulations and worker-owned cooperatives.
