Camille Tran's Cambridge-based artificial intelligence startup, ClarityLens, was acquired this week by a national data analytics firm for approximately $90 million, capping a five-year run in which the company grew from a two-person operation into a 75-employee business serving biotech and financial services clients.

Tran founded ClarityLens after leaving a data science role at a Boston-area financial firm, building software that helps organizations identify errors and inconsistencies in large datasets before they get used in reporting or compliance filings. The company built its early client base almost entirely among Boston financial services firms before expanding into biotech data clients.

"We built this company slowly and mostly on referrals, which isn't the fastest way to grow, but it meant every client we had actually needed what we built," Tran said. "Getting an acquisition offer this size after bootstrapping the first three years without outside investment still feels surreal."

Under terms of the deal, ClarityLens will continue operating out of its Cambridge office and retain its current staff, according to Tran, who will stay on as a general manager under the acquiring company for at least two years as part of the transition agreement. The acquiring firm said it was drawn to ClarityLens's established relationships with Boston financial services clients, a customer base it has struggled to penetrate on its own.

Tran said she plans to use a portion of the proceeds to fund a scholarship program for Cambridge students pursuing computer science degrees, an area she said personally mattered to her as a first-generation college graduate.
