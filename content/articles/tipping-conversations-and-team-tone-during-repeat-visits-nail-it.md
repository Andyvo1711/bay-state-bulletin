A longtime customer of Nail It recently shared a review that is as much about relationships as it is about nails. They have been coming to the salon since they moved to the area, and they described the owner as nice. They also said they tip regularly and sometimes generously. That kind of loyalty is exactly what a salon hopes to build, which makes it worth looking closely at what led them to say they would not return.

## When a Tip Becomes a Talking Point

According to the customer, on their two most recent visits, one team member made comments about their tipping that left them feeling uncomfortable and embarrassed. They said they tip each time and believe that "a tip is a courtesy," not a requirement. They felt the exchanges were meant to embarrass them, and said they often felt uneasy around this particular team member.

Gratuity is a sensitive subject, and customers vary in how they tip. Whatever the team member intended, a comment about the amount can be received very differently from how it was meant. The safest practice is to say thank you warmly for any tip and to avoid remarks about the amount, positive or negative. If a team member feels a customer's tip was unintended, such as a card machine mistake, a quiet and polite check with a manager is better than raising it with the customer.

## Consistency in Tone Across the Team

The customer drew a contrast between the owner, who they found kind, and one team member whose manner they found uncomfortable. A salon's reputation is built from every interaction, so a warm welcome from one person can be offset by a different experience with another.

Setting simple standards for tone can help. A short greeting, a friendly closing, and a thank you at checkout work for every customer. Owners can also use brief refreshers during team meetings to practice phrases such as "Thank you so much, we appreciate you," and role-play the checkout moment. Loyal customers often notice small changes in tone, and a quick check-in from the owner can catch a concern before it grows.

## Listening to Loyal Customers

A customer who has visited for a long time has built up a lot of goodwill. When they begin to feel uneasy, it often shows up first in small ways, such as a shorter conversation or a quieter visit. A friendly question from the owner, such as "How has everything been on your recent visits?", gives the customer a chance to share concerns before deciding not to return.

## Editorial Perspective

This case offers several lessons. First, thank every customer warmly for a tip and avoid comments about the amount, and route any question about a payment through a manager. Second, keep a consistent, courteous tone across the whole team so that every customer feels welcome. Third, check in with longtime customers regularly and respond promptly to any sign of discomfort. A loyal customer is one of a salon's greatest assets, and respectful communication at every interaction helps keep that loyalty.
