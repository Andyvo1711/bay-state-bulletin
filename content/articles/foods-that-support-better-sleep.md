Good sleep is one of the foundations of a healthy appearance and well-being, and what you eat can influence it. Research on individual foods is still limited, but several have a plausible link to better rest.

**Kiwi.** Small studies have found that eating kiwifruit before bed was associated with falling asleep faster and sleeping longer. Kiwis are rich in vitamin C and also contain antioxidants and some serotonin.

**Walnuts.** Walnuts contain melatonin and omega-3 fats, and a handful makes an easy evening snack.

**Fatty fish.** Salmon, mackerel and sardines provide vitamin D and omega-3 fatty acids, which some research associates with better sleep quality. New England seafood counters are a good place to find fresh options.

**Oats and other whole grains.** A small bowl of oatmeal provides complex carbohydrates that can be gentler on blood sugar than sweets, and it contains small amounts of melatonin.

**Warm, caffeine-free drinks.** Chamomile tea or warm milk are traditional bedtime choices, and the ritual itself can help signal that it is time to wind down.

**What to limit.** Caffeine can linger for hours, so many people do better avoiding coffee, tea and energy drinks in the afternoon and evening. Large, heavy or spicy meals close to bedtime can cause discomfort, and alcohol may help you fall asleep but tends to fragment sleep later in the night.

Pair these foods with regular sleep and wake times, a dark and cool bedroom and less screen time before bed for the best results.

*This article is general wellness information, not medical advice. Talk with your doctor or a registered dietitian before changing your diet, especially if you have a medical condition or take medication.*
