A client who paid $80 for a full set with dip powder at Nail Tech found herself without any nails left just three days later. She described it as the weakest set she has ever had and questioned why she paid that much for something that did not last. While a short review like this leaves out some of the finer details of the visit, it points clearly to a durability problem that is worth examining closely, especially given the price point involved.

## Looking at the Application Process

Dip powder sets rely on careful, layered application to build strength, and shortcuts anywhere in that process tend to show up as early failure rather than an immediate visible flaw. Each layer of powder needs to be properly activated and allowed to set before the next one is added, and rushing through these steps to move faster through a busy schedule can leave the set structurally weaker than it looks. Reviewing the full application sequence, including how many layers are used and how much time is given between them, is a good starting point when a set fails this quickly.

## Preparing the Natural Nail Underneath

As with other extension and powder methods, the durability of a dip powder set depends heavily on how well the natural nail is prepped beforehand. Any oil, moisture, or leftover product on the nail plate can prevent the powder from bonding securely, even when the top layers look smooth and well finished. A full set that comes off entirely within days, rather than showing gradual lifting at the edges, often points back to this kind of adhesion issue at the base rather than a problem with the topcoat or design work on top.

## Matching Price to Durability Expectations

At $80, this client reasonably expected a set that would hold up for at least a few weeks, which is a standard expectation for dip powder services at that price range. When a set fails within days, the gap between the price paid and the result delivered becomes the most visible part of the complaint, even more than the technical cause. Salons charging premium prices for dip powder sets should be confident the underlying technique can consistently support that price point, and any early failure like this is worth investigating right away rather than treating as an isolated incident.

## Editorial Perspective

This case offers a few clear lessons for salons working with dip powder services. First, each layer of a dip powder application needs proper time and technique to build a durable set, and skipping steps to save time often shows up as failure within days rather than weeks. Second, thorough nail prep before the first layer goes on is just as important for dip powder as it is for gel or acrylic extensions, since a compromised base undermines everything applied on top of it. Third, pricing a service at a premium level comes with an expectation of matching durability, and a client paying $80 for a set that lasts only three days is a strong signal that something in the process needs a closer look.
