Economic activity in the First District, which covers New England other than Connecticut, expanded slightly, with mixed results across sectors, according to the Federal Reserve Bank of Boston's October 14 Beige Book. The report's headline said the outlook dimmed on cost concerns.

Consumer spending was flat in Boston, with an uptick in tourism spending but softer retail sales, and higher-end spending stayed strong. Boston-area staffing contacts said workers were motivated to seek new jobs because wage increases lagged inflation. The September report had already described a more pessimistic outlook amid inflation concerns.

A separate Boston Fed memo, New England Economic Conditions through October 13, said the unemployment rate was 4.1% in both New England and the nation in August, and that inflation eased to 3.4% in both. Payroll employment grew 0.3% year over year in New England in August.
