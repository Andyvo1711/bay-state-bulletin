The Port of Baltimore handled a record volume of container cargo last year, according to figures released by the Maryland Port Administration, marking a full recovery and continued growth in shipping traffic since operations were disrupted following the Francis Scott Key Bridge collapse.

Port officials said container volume climbed roughly 9 percent compared with the prior full year, driven by continued strength in automobile imports, a category where Baltimore has long ranked among the busiest ports in the country, along with growing volume in specialized cargo such as forest products and machinery.

"We went through a period where a lot of shipping lines had to reroute cargo to other East Coast ports out of necessity, and the fact that we've not just recovered that business but grown beyond it says a lot about the relationships this port has with shipping lines," said the Maryland Port Administration's executive director. "Cargo doesn't come back automatically just because a channel reopens. Customers had to choose to come back."

The port's automobile handling operations, historically one of its strongest business lines, posted particularly strong growth as several manufacturers expanded import volumes through Baltimore's specialized vehicle-handling terminals. Port officials said continued federal investment in channel dredging has also allowed larger vessels to call at Baltimore's terminals than in previous years.

The Maryland Port Administration said the record volume supports tens of thousands of direct and indirect jobs tied to port operations across the Baltimore region, from longshoremen to trucking and warehousing employment. State officials have continued to emphasize the port's economic recovery as a marker of the broader region bouncing back from the bridge collapse's disruption to shipping and commuter traffic.
