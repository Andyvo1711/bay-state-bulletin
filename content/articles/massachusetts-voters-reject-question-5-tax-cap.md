Massachusetts voters rejected Question 5, which would have changed how the state calculates its annual tax-revenue limit. The campaign behind the measure conceded and the Associated Press projected it would fail on November 5. With about 86% of votes counted, 52.2% voted no.

Under current law, if collections exceed the limit, the excess is returned to taxpayers. The measure would have tied the limit to the net amount of state revenue from the prior year, increased by the average growth of wages and salaries in Massachusetts over the most recent three years, and would have counted all revenue from the surtax on incomes over $1 million. Supporters said it would make refunds more likely; critics said it would put a strict limit on state revenue and require repeated budget cuts.

With the measure defeated, the existing formula stays in place. The limit has been exceeded only twice, in 1987 and 2022. A certified final tally was not available at publication.
