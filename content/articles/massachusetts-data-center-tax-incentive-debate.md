Massachusetts lawmakers are debating new tax incentives aimed at attracting large-scale data center development to the state, an effort to compete with neighboring states that have offered more aggressive tax exemptions on computer equipment purchases.

The proposed legislation would exempt data center operators from state sales tax on equipment purchases, mirroring incentives other states have offered for years, in hopes of capturing some of the enormous capital investment flowing into data center construction driven by growing demand for cloud computing and artificial intelligence infrastructure.

"We've watched an enormous amount of investment go right past us for years now, largely because our tax treatment of this industry just isn't competitive," said a state senator sponsoring the incentive legislation. "This bill is an attempt to get Massachusetts into that conversation instead of watching from the sidelines."

Critics of the proposal, including some environmental advocates and fiscal watchdog groups, have raised concerns about the significant electricity and water consumption associated with large data centers, along with questions about whether the relatively modest permanent job creation from data center operations justifies the scale of tax exemptions typically required to attract such projects, particularly given the state's already constrained electricity grid.

Data center industry representatives testified that Massachusetts's existing tax structure and high electricity costs have made the state largely uncompetitive for large-scale data center site selection, despite the state's dense fiber infrastructure. Lawmakers said they expect a vote on the incentive package before the current legislative session concludes, though the bill's future remains uncertain given the mixed reception from environmental and fiscal policy groups.
