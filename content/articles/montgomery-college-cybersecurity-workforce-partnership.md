Montgomery College has launched a new cybersecurity workforce partnership with several federal agencies and defense contractors based in the Washington suburbs, aiming to move students directly from a two-year associate degree into entry-level security clearance-eligible jobs.

The program, developed with input from agencies headquartered in Montgomery County, will restructure the college's existing cybersecurity curriculum around specific technical certifications that federal contractors say they struggle to find enough candidates for, including cloud security and network defense credentials.

"We kept hearing from employers that they had open positions they couldn't fill, and students who wanted those jobs but didn't know exactly which skills to build," said the director of Montgomery College's cybersecurity program. "This partnership is really about closing that gap directly instead of guessing what employers want."

Under the new agreement, participating employers will offer paid internships to students in their second year of the program, with several committing to interview all students who complete the internship and meet basic clearance eligibility requirements. College officials said roughly 150 students are expected to enroll in the restructured program in its first year.

Montgomery County officials have praised the partnership as a model for connecting community college training directly to the region's dense concentration of federal and defense-related employers. Montgomery College said it plans to expand the model to other technical programs, including data analytics and network administration, if the cybersecurity partnership proves successful.
