Each autumn, cranberry bogs in southeastern Massachusetts and on Cape Cod are flooded for harvest, one of New England's best-known seasonal sights. Massachusetts is among the leading cranberry-growing states in the country.

**What they offer.** Fresh cranberries are low in calories and provide fiber, vitamin C and polyphenol antioxidants. They are very tart, which is why most cranberry products are sweetened.

**Watch the sugar.** Many dried cranberries and cranberry juice cocktails contain a lot of added sugar. Look for unsweetened or lightly sweetened products, or cook fresh berries with a small amount of sweetener, orange zest and cinnamon.

**Easy ways to enjoy them.** Stir chopped fresh cranberries into oatmeal or plain Greek yogurt, add them to roasted squash or a grain salad, or simmer them into a simple sauce. Fresh berries freeze well, so you can buy extra during harvest season.

**Pair with other fall produce.** Apples, pears, squash and sweet potatoes bring fiber and carotenoids and round out a seasonal menu.

**About urinary health.** Cranberry products have been studied for preventing urinary tract infections in some people, with mixed results, and they are not a treatment for an active infection. See a clinician if you have symptoms. Cranberries can also interact with some blood thinners, so ask your doctor if you take warfarin.

*This article is general wellness information, not medical advice. Talk with your doctor or a registered dietitian before changing your diet, especially if you have a medical condition or take medication.*
