Mass General Brigham began a new round of layoffs in mid-October and is significantly scaling back its Home Hospital program, the Boston Globe reported.

The system began layoffs on October 13 and has not said whether the total will reach hundreds or thousands, because "many decisions have not yet been made." Mass General Brigham cut about 1,500 positions in two waves in 2025. Citing rising costs, it said reimbursements from Medicare, Medicaid and commercial payers are not keeping pace with expenses.

The Globe reported the nine-year-old Home Hospital program had lost about $30 million a year on average over the past three years. Clinical operations were paused indefinitely at Salem Hospital, Newton-Wellesley Hospital and Brigham and Women's, and the program will be refocused on Mass General and Brigham. A spokesperson said the changes create "a more focused, sustainable service" and that the program is being redesigned rather than eliminated. Pediatric inpatient care is also shifting, with Mass Eye and Ear's pediatric inpatients moving to other Mass General Brigham for Children locations.
