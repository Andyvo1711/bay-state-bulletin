The Boston Globe published a series on student absenteeism beginning November 5, based on years of daily attendance data, surveys of the state's nearly 300 public school superintendents, a poll of parents, and interviews with more than a hundred researchers, school leaders, students, teachers and families.

The series found that the surge in chronic absenteeism has eased since the pandemic, but recovery has been slow: last year's rate remained about one-third higher than in 2019. In a Suffolk University/Boston Globe poll, half of Massachusetts parents who responded said they have taken their children out of school to travel for a vacation or holiday. Superintendents named mental health days among the leading contributors to absences, according to the Globe's follow-up coverage.

A November 15 installment of the series said more students are missing school across demographic groups.
