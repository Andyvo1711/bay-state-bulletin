Skincare products get most of the attention, but diet plays a supporting role in how skin looks and functions. No food replaces sun protection, but several nutrients are associated with healthier skin.

**Fatty fish.** Salmon, mackerel and sardines supply omega-3 fatty acids, which help maintain the skin's oil barrier and have anti-inflammatory effects.

**Colorful fruits and vegetables.** Carrots, sweet potatoes, squash and leafy greens contain carotenoids that the body converts to vitamin A or stores in skin, and some studies link higher intake with a warmer skin tone. Berries and citrus provide vitamin C, which the body needs to make collagen.

**Nuts and seeds.** Almonds, sunflower seeds and walnuts provide vitamin E and healthy fats.

**Olive oil and avocado.** Monounsaturated fats are part of Mediterranean-style eating patterns associated with better overall health.

**Water and hydrating foods.** Staying hydrated supports overall health; cucumbers, oranges and soups contribute to fluid intake.

**What to limit.** Diets very high in sugar and highly processed foods are associated in some studies with more skin inflammation and acne in some people, though research is mixed.

Most skin changes come from sun exposure and genetics, so daily broad-spectrum sunscreen, not smoking and gentle skincare remain the most effective steps. See a dermatologist for persistent acne, rashes or any changing mole.

*This article is general wellness information, not medical advice. Talk with your doctor or a registered dietitian before changing your diet, especially if you have a medical condition or take medication.*
