Hair and nails are built mostly from keratin, a protein, so a diet that provides enough protein and key minerals supports their growth.

**Eggs.** Eggs supply protein and biotin, a B vitamin involved in keratin production. True biotin deficiency is uncommon in people who eat a varied diet.

**Fish and shellfish.** Salmon provides protein and omega-3 fats, and oysters are among the richest food sources of zinc, which is involved in tissue growth and repair.

**Beans, lentils and leafy greens.** These provide iron and protein; low iron is a common reason for hair shedding, particularly in menstruating women, so persistent hair loss is worth discussing with a doctor.

**Nuts and seeds.** Pumpkin seeds, almonds and walnuts supply zinc, vitamin E and healthy fats.

**Citrus and berries.** Vitamin C helps the body absorb iron from plant foods and supports collagen formation.

**A caution on supplements.** High-dose biotin products are heavily marketed for hair and nails, but evidence of benefit is limited in people who are not deficient, and biotin can interfere with some lab tests, including thyroid and heart-attack blood tests. Tell your clinician if you take it.

If you notice sudden hair loss, ridges, spooning or discoloration of nails, see a clinician, because these can signal medical or nutritional issues.

*This article is general wellness information, not medical advice. Talk with your doctor or a registered dietitian before changing your diet, especially if you have a medical condition or take medication.*
