Massachusetts's manufacturing employment is shifting significantly toward defense and precision components, according to state labor department figures, as suppliers tied to defense contracts and robotics expand hiring even as some traditional manufacturing segments face slower demand.

Worcester County and the state's precision manufacturing corridor, home to a concentration of defense and aerospace component suppliers, have seen steady hiring growth, while manufacturers focused on more commodity-oriented production have faced softer demand and, in some cases, workforce reductions.

"Massachusetts manufacturing has always had to specialize in higher-value, higher-precision work because we can't compete on labor cost with other regions, and that specialization is paying off right now given how much defense and robotics demand has grown," said a state labor economist who tracks manufacturing employment trends. "The suppliers who made that bet years ago are the ones hiring."

State officials said they are monitoring the situation closely, noting that the employment picture varies significantly by county and by which specific manufacturing segment a given supplier serves. Economic development officials said the state has been working to attract more defense and robotics manufacturers directly, pointing to several recent facility expansion announcements as evidence of progress.

Labor officials cautioned against reading too much into short-term county-level data, noting that Massachusetts's overall manufacturing employment has remained relatively stable even as the composition of that employment shifts toward higher-precision, defense-oriented work. State economic development officials said they expect the shift toward specialized manufacturing to continue as the state's traditional lower-margin manufacturers continue facing competitive pressure.
